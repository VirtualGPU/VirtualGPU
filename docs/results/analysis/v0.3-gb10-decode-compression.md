# Why GB10 decode predictions are compressed: location and a registered test

**Post-hoc diagnosis, then a registration.** On main `f1f8b92`, whose core is identical to `70de487`. The diagnosis reads KernelIR and profiles only: it reads no label, sealed or not. The measured side is quoted from the published post-hoc analysis. The registration is fixed before any run, for review by 6 and release by the lead. Nothing here changes the core, a profile or a rule.

ROADMAP item: "GB10 decode shapes are predicted too compressed: find out why".

## What is compressed

`decode-16` is `(M, N, K) = (16, 8192, 4096)`, 36 configs. From [posthoc-core-phase1-phase2.md](posthoc-core-phase1-phase2.md), section 2 (phase 2 labels, profile `0.11.0-m3`), the measured times fall into three groups:

| Group | Measured (us) |
|---|---|
| small tiles, `num_stages = 4` (prefetch distance 2) | 270 to 275 |
| `num_stages = 1/2` (prefetch distance 0), and 128-row tiles with one CTA per SM | 290 to 301 |
| `m128n64k64-w8-s1/s2` | 325 to 330 |

## Facts on main and the 0.23.0 profile

KernelIR: the phase 2 development AOT of `decode-16` (36 configs, the IR that [the form c analysis](v0.3-gb10-h3-h4-posthoc.md) used). Core `70de487`, profile `gb10-0.23.0`, draw 0.

1. **Predictions.** Warm, 33 of 36 configs are predicted at 281.0 to 282.0 us. The other three:
   - `m128n64k64-w8-s1` at 295.5 us;
   - `m128n64k64-w8-s2` at 295.0 us;
   - `m128n128k64-w4-s4` at 282.0 us.

   The spread is 5.2%, against 22% measured. Cold is the same within 0.5 us.
2. **Every config is DRAM bandwidth bound.** All 36 report `dram_bandwidth` as the bound for 0.88 to 1.00 of their time. They deliver 239.8 GB/s, the `dram_bw_by_active_sms` ceiling at 48 SMs (240.1 GB/s). The DRAM bytes are 67.4 MB for every config.
3. **The latency path gives the same time.** With `dram_bw_by_active_sms` doubled (a counterfactual model input, nothing else changed), the bound moves to `dram_latency` for every config. The times are 277.9 to 293.8 us, the same spread. The latency comes from the DRAM MLP curve by Little's law (bytes in flight over the curve's bandwidth at that in flight). The configs run at 239 to 243 GB/s, the curve's saturated plateau. On that plateau the latency grows in proportion to the bytes in flight, so the time comes out as bytes over the plateau bandwidth whatever the tile, warps or stages. That last step is an inference from the identical times, not a separate measurement.
4. **Not the port floor.** Core `9c12720` (before the round-trip port floor), with the same doubled input, gives 277.9 to 291.0 us. The floor changes no config by more than 1.0%.
5. **Not the low in-flight latency states, for 30 configs.** The 32 MLP points flagged `low_in_flight_unstable` (4 to 256 KiB in flight, both issue modes) were scaled by 0.8 as a counterfactual. 30 configs did not move, and two moved by 0.1 us. Four configs moved:
   - `m128n64k64-w8-s1/s2`: 295 to 307 us;
   - `m128n128k64-w4/w8-s4`: 282 to 287 and 284 us.

   These are the configs that already stand apart, with partial waves and one or two CTAs per SM. The band can widen the spread at the slow end. It does not separate the 270 us group from the 290 us group.
6. **The contiguous closed loop has no prefetch gap at these grids.** `dram_closed_loop` in 0.23.0 (long launches, five rounds), `cp_async`, no compute, at decode-16's sizes:

   | CTAs | Stage | Delivered GB/s for 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 buffers |
   |---|---|---|
   | 96 | 4 KiB | 241.3 / 241.0 / 240.2 / 239.6 |
   | 96 | 8 KiB | 240.3 / 240.1 / 239.8 / 239.6 |
   | 128 | 4 KiB | 241.5 / 240.5 / 239.7 / 237.8 |
   | 128 | 8 KiB | 237.1 / 238.3 / 237.6 / 237.4 |

   This is the finding of the phase 2 post-hoc, now on the qualified profile: the measured 7% to 10% gap between prefetch distance 0 and 2 is not in the contiguous microbenchmark.
7. **Access granularity is measured, as an MLP curve, and not read by the core.** `decode-16` reads `B` (row stride 16 KiB) in chunks of `BLOCK_N * 2` bytes: 128 B for `n64` and 256 B for `n128`. The profile's `dram_bw_by_bytes_in_flight_strided` has the shape. Its plateau at 128 B segments is 221 to 223 GB/s, at 512 B 233 to 235, and at 4 KiB 236 to 239, against 242 for contiguous streams. No core code reads the curve. Read by the core, it would move all configs by about 8%, not spread them, because every config reads 128 or 256 B chunks.

## Location

The compression is located in the DRAM MLP curve's saturated plateau. It is not in the port floor or in the low in-flight band (facts 3 to 5). Every `decode-16` config is past the knee. There the core's DRAM time, by bandwidth share or by Little's law latency, is the bytes over one plateau bandwidth. No input of the core depends on the prefetch distance there. The measured groups split by prefetch distance (the table above), and the only contiguous measurement of that variable shows no effect (fact 6).

## Hypothesis

**H-gran.** On GB10, with `decode-16`'s access, the delivered DRAM bandwidth depends on the stage buffers per CTA at equal grid and stage size. The access is chunks of 128 to 256 B at a 16 KiB stride. The bandwidth is lower with one buffer (prefetch distance 0) than with four. With contiguous stages it does not depend on the buffers (fact 6).

H-gran combines facts 6 and 7. Neither measurement covers the strided access in a closed loop. That is the gap the test fills.

## Registered test

A diagnostic mode, not in `BUILD_MODES` and not in any profile: the `closed-loop` kernel with the `strided-mlp` access pattern. Each CTA's stage is `segment` bytes from each of `stage / segment` rows at a fixed stride, and the phase advances on wrap as in `strided_mlp_cp_async_kernel`, so there is no L2 reuse.

- **Grid.** 96 and 128 CTAs. Stages of 4 and 8 KiB. Buffers 1, 2 and 4. `cp_async`, no compute.
- **Access.** Segments of 128 and 256 B at a 16 KiB stride (strided), and contiguous stages (the control). All interleaved within each process, in a fixed order per process: grid, stage, access, buffers ascending.
- **Points.** 2 x 2 x 3 x 3 = 36.
- **Lengths.** Pilot sized to in-kernel windows of at least 2.5 ms, twice the observe overload. This follows the carveout re-registration, because strided points near a level switch can shorten a window.
- **Acquisition.** GB10 on Spark 1, under the GPU lock for the transfer, the build and every run, coordinated with 7 and 8. Three processes, each through `capture_posthoc.py` with `--clock-probe`, the thermal and host gates, and rules 1 and 2. A process that fails a gate, `long_launch.verify`, or rule 1 or 2 is void, archived, not read, and replaced, at most twice. Rule 3 is reported, not used to void.

**Statistic.** For each (grid, stage, access): the gap g = bw(4 buffers) / bw(1 buffer) - 1, from the median over the three processes of each point's median.

**Readings, fixed here.**

- **Control.** On the contiguous access, |g| must be at most 2% at all four (grid, stage) pairs, as in fact 6. If not, the control is not reproduced and nothing is read.
- **H-gran supported.** g >= 5% at at least three of the four (grid, stage) pairs, for at least one of the two segment sizes.
- **H-gran rejected.** |g| < 2% at all eight strided pairs.
- **Inconclusive.** Every other outcome. All gaps are reported.

**Reported, not judged.** Every point's bandwidth and round range, g per pair, the 2-buffer points, and the strided plateau against the profile's `dram_bw_by_bytes_in_flight_strided`.

**What each reading leads to (not decided here).**

- Supported: a later registration of a core form that reads a closed-loop bandwidth by access granularity. It is scored first on this microbenchmark and then on decode GEMM labels.
- Rejected: the next suspect is something no microbenchmark has isolated, such as tail waves at one or two CTAs per SM (fact 5) or the GEMM's own instruction stream. Prefetch distance at strided access is then excluded.

## Data the item still needs, beyond this test

- **Labels.** The per-config `decode-16` labels on GB10 are in the sealed phase 2 reports, and are not read here. Linking a microbenchmark result to the GEMM needs one of two things, and the choice is the lead's:
  - a logged post-hoc access through `validation/posthoc.py`;
  - a new, open GB10 decode development capture under the long-launch protocol.
- **Age of the groups.** The measured groups are from short launches on the `0.11.0-m3` era setup. Whether they hold under long launches is not known.

## Limits

- One device. The RTX 4070 `decode-16` is not compressed (tau-b 0.697 warm on the v0.2 development baseline, profile `rtx4070` `0.21.0-posthoc`), so this item is GB10-only. The RTX 4070 run of the same mode would serve as a second control, not as a test.
- The counterfactuals of facts 3 and 5 change model inputs only. They show where the core's time comes from, not what the hardware does.
- H-gran covers the 270 us against 290 us split. The 325 to 330 us group (`m128n64k64-w8-s1/s2`) may need its own explanation.

## Result: control not reproduced, no reading

Measurement commit `65eef36` (local branch `s4/v03-strided-closed-loop`, not in main), binary SHA-256 `840e9b18118470ecc6547d63a071e85654aa4193cca7e7187e39c2dba7eedd6c`, built on Spark 1 with CUDA 13.0 for `sm_121a`.

- **Session.** 2026-11-27 from 22:46:51 to 22:54:02 UTC, under the GPU lock for the transfer, the build and every run. The pilot froze 36 points at 22:48:29.
- **Raw records.** `results/v03-gb10-decode-closed-loop/65eef36/spark-raw.tar.gz` (SHA-256 `36533f13...`) in the 4 worktree.
- **Processes.** The first three were valid, and no replacement was needed. Each passed the thermal and host gates, rules 1 and 2 (process 3: 2 of 72 lists over the IQR ratio, 3 allowed), and `long_launch.verify` (shortest launch 2.47 ms).
- **Rule 3, reported.** The largest spread of a point's per-process medians is 6.3%. No point is over 10%.

**The control is not reproduced.** On the contiguous access, g lies outside plus or minus 2% at three of the four (grid, stage) pairs: -4.7% (96 CTAs, 4 KiB), -3.7% (96, 8 KiB) and +4.2% (128, 8 KiB). At (128, 4 KiB) it is -1.1%. By the registered rule, nothing is read, and no statement on H-gran is made.

- **Strided gaps.** g at the eight strided pairs is -0.2% to +0.5%. The strided plateaus are near the profile's `dram_bw_by_bytes_in_flight_strided`: 128 B 225 here against 221 to 223 there, 256 B 233 here (the profile has 512 B at 233 to 235).
- **What the control shows.** Each contiguous point sits at one of two levels, about 240 or 228 to 232 GB/s, and mostly keeps it across the three processes. The level does not follow the buffer count. The same points in the 0.23.0 `dram_closed_loop` (fact 6) are 237 to 241 for every buffer count.
- **Likely cause, not checked.** Both flush L2 before every launch. This replica differs from the profile's closed loop in ways the registration did not foresee:
  - a 4 GiB source buffer instead of 256 MiB;
  - launches of 2.5 ms instead of about 1.3 ms;
  - three processes instead of five rounds, and a different point order.

  The profile's points also reach the lower level in some rounds (their round median ranges go down to 232 to 236 GB/s), and the median of five rounds sits on the upper one. Which difference, if any, sets the level is not known.

Whether to repeat with a control that matches the profile's closed loop, and how, is the lead's decision. Repeating needs a new registration.
