# How the core charges barrier synchronization: amendment

Status: amends [the registration](v0.3-barrier-sync-form-registration.md) before any capture under it. It corrects one statement of fact, found while implementing the test. The new points, the gate and the pass rule are unchanged.

## The correction

The registration says that the microbenchmarks of `fixed_sync_ns`, `stage_sync_overhead_ns` and `sync_stage_loop` have one barrier per step. In the source (`microbench/src/m2_kernels.cuh`):

- `fixed_sync_empty_kernel` and both `stage_overhead` kernels have one `__syncthreads()` per step.
- `sync_stage_loop_kernel` has two `bar.sync` per step: one after the stage is loaded and one after the MMA chain.

## What follows

- **(a) The regression check still holds, for another reason.** The registration's check is that the form leaves the existing microbenchmarks' predictions unchanged. It still does, but not because those kernels have one barrier per step. `scripts/recheck_mechanisms.py` builds their kernel IR without a loop barrier op, carrying only `resources.barriers`. So the form reads n = 0 and charges nothing extra, for `sync_stage_loop` too.
- **(b) `sync_stage_loop` with its real barrier count is a description only.** If the IR of those points carried their two barriers per step, the form would charge each step one more barrier. Those points are in the profile's basis and their re-check errors have been seen, so this would be a post-hoc description, not a test, and nothing is judged on it.
- **The re-check's IR does not match the real kernels.** The re-check's IR carries no loop barrier op, while `sync_stage_loop_kernel` has two barriers per step and the other two kernels one. This is a fact about the re-check. Whether its IR should carry the real barrier counts is a separate question, not changed here.

The new `barrier-pipeline` points, the gate, the pass rule and the expected effect on real kernels are as registered. Their B = 2 points have the structure of `sync_stage_loop_kernel`.
