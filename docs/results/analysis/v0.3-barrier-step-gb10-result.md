By the frozen rules:

- **Reading 2, the barrier-free step.** Two configurations read "does not pay the barrier cost" (`W` = 512 at 8 warps): the first barrier adds 113 to 125 ns, far above the profile's 29.2 ns. Two read "pays it" (`W` = 512 at 4 warps): one barrier changes the step by under 5%. The other eight are not resolved: at `W` = 0 and 64 the first barrier adds 1.2 to 8.0 ns, more than 5% of `t(1)` but less than half of `fixed_sync_ns`.
- **Reading 3, the barrier count.** It matters in ten configurations and does not in two (`W` = 512 at 4 warps, where the barriers are hidden by the work).
- **Reading 4, linear in the count.** Linear at `W` = 0 (largest residual 1.6% to 4.7%) and in one of the two `W` = 512, 8-warp configurations; not linear at `W` = 64, where the first barriers are partly hidden (`t` rises by 1 to 5 ns up to 4 barriers, then by about 8 to 9 ns per barrier), nor in the other `W` = 512, 8-warp configuration (largest residual 8.1%).

## Reported without a verdict

- **Size against the profile.** On an empty step (`W` = 0) one more barrier costs 7.8 ns at 4 warps and 10.9 ns at 8 warps, about 20 and 28 SM cycles at the median measured clock (2560 MHz): about a third of `fixed_sync_ns` (26.0 and 29.2 ns), whose kernel also waits for and commits an asynchronous copy group every step.
- **One CTA against all SMs** (all SMs over one CTA, median of the processes). At `W` = 0 and 64, within 2% at every barrier count (largest -1.95%). At `W` = 512: at 4 warps +2.6% to +2.9% at 0 to 2 barriers and within 0.4% from 4 on; at 8 warps +2.5% at 2 barriers and within 2% at the others.
- **The 8-warp, 512-instruction step.** Without barriers the eight warps run out of step and the step takes 409 to 410 ns; with one barrier per step it takes 523 to 533 ns, and further barriers add about 5 ns each. One explanation is that a barrier puts all warps in step every iteration, so their FMA streams no longer overlap; this run does not test it.

## What this says for the two roadmap items

These are observations for the core's owner, not a proposal:

- The core charges `fixed_sync_ns` once per step whatever the barrier count. Here the count matters in most configurations, and on an empty step the cost grows linearly at about 8 to 11 ns per barrier.
- Whether a barrier-free loop should pay the step charge has no single answer: with enough work per warp at 4 warps the barriers cost nothing, while at 8 warps with the same work the first barrier costs about four times `fixed_sync_ns`.

The RTX 4070 part waits for a quiet desktop window.
