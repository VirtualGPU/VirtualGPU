# Which carveout a kernel with no shared memory gets: registered probe

Registered on `1af5351` before any implementation or run, for review by 6 and release by the lead. Nothing below changes after the data are taken. It measures; it changes no core, profile or rule. Any fix is left to a later PR after the spill decision of `ad1d505` is scored (issue #21, option (a)).

## Question

The core reads the L1 miss rate of spill frames from the curve of the smallest measured carveout at or above the kernel's carveout (`spill_miss_rate` in `crates/vgpu-core/src/l2/mod.rs`). It computes that carveout in `occupancy` as the smallest option that holds the resident CTAs' shared memory, including the 1 KiB reserved per CTA. For the `spill-latency` kernel (no dynamic shared memory) this gives 8 KiB, so the 16 KiB curve is read. That curve misses almost fully at a 128 KiB footprint. The zero curve misses 0.56 (GB10) there, and with it the `l1_boundary` batch points are predicted with MAPE 13.1% instead of 54.9% (the correction of `v0.3-gb10-first-capture-l2-diagnosis.md`, section 8, local commit `7d92e2c`, under review).

The curves come from `run_l1_carveout`: one 1024-thread CTA with a shared memory request of 0, 16, 32, 48, 64 or 99 KiB, and a `ld.global.ca` pointer chase. So a curve is labelled by the request, while the core looks it up by the carveout it computes from need plus reserve. Two readings are possible:

- **Labelled by request.** The driver gives a no-request kernel the L1 of the zero curve, and the core should look up by request.
- **Labelled by carveout.** The driver gives such a kernel an L1 like the 16 KiB curve, or the core's reading holds otherwise.

Which one holds is not measured. This probe measures it on GB10 and decides no fix.

## Step 1: L1 cliff by launch shape

A diagnostic mode, not in `BUILD_MODES` and not in any profile, runs `l1_carveout_pchase_kernel` unchanged, with one CTA, in four launch configurations interleaved within each process:

| Name | Threads per CTA | Shared memory request | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| R0 | 1024 | 0 | replica of the zero curve |
| R8 | 1024 | 8 KiB | the core's computed carveout |
| R16 | 1024 | 16 KiB | the curve the core reads |
| S | 128 | 0, no carveout attribute | the `spill-latency` CTA shape |

- **Working sets.** 16, 32 and 64 KiB (the L1 reference), 96 to 160 KiB in 4 KiB steps, and 192, 224, 256 and 320 KiB (the L2 reference).
- **Lengths.** Chase lengths are sized by the pilot so that each in-kernel window is at least 1.25 ms (the observe overload), and every capture window is at least 1.0 ms.
- **Cliff.** The cliff C of a configuration is computed exactly as `collect_profile.py` computes `threshold_bytes_per_sm`. Per sample: L1 latency is the median of the first three points, L2 the median of the last four, and the cliff is where the latency first crosses their midpoint, interpolated. C is the median over samples.

## Step 2: the `spill-latency` kernel's own transition

The spill kernel indexes its frame with a power-of-two mask, so the footprint per SM is varied with the warps per CTA, not with an odd frame.

- **Shape.** Grid one CTA per SM, batched reloads (`batch_before_dep`), 128 reloads, frames of 128 B, 512 B and 4096 B, and 6, 7, 8, 9 and 10 warps per CTA. At 512 B these are footprints of 96, 112, 128, 144 and 160 KiB per SM.
- **Normalized miss.** At each warp count w, m(w) = (t512 - t128) / (t4096 - t128) from the per-step times at the three frames. The 128 B frame stays in L1 and the 4096 B frame in L2, at the same warps.
- **Transition.** T is the footprint where m crosses 0.5, interpolated over w.
- **Allocations.** The reload points' level depends on the local memory allocation (8's diagnostic, `gb10-spill-alloc-plan.md`). So step 2 runs in 5 new contexts (`cudaDeviceReset`, default stack limit) per process, all three frames and five warp counts in each context. T is computed per context and reported per context, and the reading uses the median of the per-context T. Within a context the points run in a fixed order: warps ascending, frames 4096, 512, 128 B.
- **Lengths.** Steps are sized by the pilot to launches of at least 1.25 ms, as in `spill-latency`.

## Acquisition

GB10 on Spark 1, under the GPU lock. Three processes in one session, each through `capture_posthoc.py` with `--clock-probe`, the thermal and host gates, and rules 1 and 2 on its lists. A process that fails a gate or rule 1 or 2 is void, archived and replaced, at most twice; the readings use the first three valid processes. Rule 3 is reported, not used to void.

## Readings, fixed here

**Reference.** C(R0) must lie within 8 KiB of the GB10 profile's zero-carveout threshold (128378 B). If it does not, the reference is not reproduced and nothing is read.

The readings use the medians over the three processes:

- **Labelled by request:** |C(S) - C(R0)| <= 4 KiB and |C(S) - C(R16)| > 8 KiB.
- **Labelled by carveout:** |C(S) - C(R16)| <= 4 KiB, or |C(S) - C(R8)| <= 4 KiB with |C(R8) - C(R0)| > 8 KiB.
- **Neither:** every other outcome. All four cliffs are reported and no labelling is named.

Step 2 is read against step 1:

- **Consistent:** |T - C(S)| <= 8 KiB.
- **Inconsistent:** otherwise. Both values are reported and step 1's reading stands only as a statement about the pointer chase.

Reported, not judged: C for every configuration and process, m(w) per context, and the per-context T.

## Limits

- One device, GB10. The RTX 4070 would need its own run.
- The driver's carveout choice may depend on more than the request and the CTA size, for example the registers or the occupancy the kernel could reach. Configuration S covers the `spill-latency` shape only.
- Step 2's warp counts also change how many reloads are in flight. The normalization at each warp count is meant to remove that, not proven to.
