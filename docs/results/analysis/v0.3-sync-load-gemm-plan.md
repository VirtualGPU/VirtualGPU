# Plan: the missing mechanism of synchronous-load GEMM on GB10 (registration)

**Plan, not a result.** Registered before any code or capture, for the `ROADMAP.md` item "Synchronous load GEMM (`num_stages = 1`, +9.1% warm): find the missing mechanism with a microbenchmark". It produces measurements only; a core change, if any, is a separate pull request.

## The residual

GEMM configs with `num_stages = 1` are predicted slow on the development set: median error +9.1% warm and +2.2% cold (core `7b1be46`, profile `0.21.0`; [design.md 9.2](../../design.md#92-v02-residuals-and-exclusions)). The core treats them as synchronous loads, prefetch distance 0: each stage costs a round trip plus its compute, hidden only across the CTAs of an SM ([model-core.md](../../components/model-core.md)).

## Explanations already ruled out

From [design.md 9.1 and 9.2](../../design.md#91-phase-1-residuals-on-the-rtx-4070) and the [post-hoc look at phases 1 and 2](posthoc-core-phase1-phase2.md), each by a microbenchmark without GEMM timings:

- MMA chain fill and drain (`mma_chain_latency`); shared memory to register latency (`serial_stage_overhead_ns`); serial shared memory fill as an addition to the round trip (`serial_stage_fill`); tensor, issue, shared memory port and scheduler contention between co-resident CTAs (`dram_closed_loop_mma`); register spills (none reported).
- A register-staging cost the model misses: on `sync_stage_loop` with two or three CTAs per SM, register-staged loops are predicted with MAPE 4.2% (RTX 4070) and 8.3% (GB10); with one CTA per SM the model keeps the shared memory stores on the CTA's chain, and taking them off makes it worse.
- Round trips in phase on the port: `7b1be46` shares the port out of phase, as `sync_stage_loop` with 1 to 8 CTAs per SM shows.

## What the kernels do (checked for this plan, CPU only)

The validation GEMM kernel (`dense_gemm` in `validation/runner.py`) compiled by Triton 3.9.0 for `sm_121` and `sm_89` (2048 cubed, `BM x BN x BK` = 128 x 128 x 64 and 64 x 64 x 32, 4 warps, 16-byte aligned pointers):

- `num_stages = 1` loads with `ld.global.v4.b32`, the default cache operator, which allocates in L1. `num_stages` 2 and 3 load with `cp.async.cg`, which bypasses L1.
- In the `num_stages = 1` loop the assembler issues the A loads, a barrier, the B loads, then all shared memory stores, a barrier, and the `ldmatrix` and MMA work; the loop starts with the loads and ends with the MMAs. No load of the next iteration is issued before the current MMAs.

So one candidate is ruled out here: the compiler does not prefetch the next stage through registers, and the barrier between the A and B loads can only make the real loop slower, the opposite of the residual.

## Candidates not ruled out

1. **L1 reuse of synchronous loads (tested here).** Only the synchronous path's loads can hit in L1. CTAs on one SM that read the same A or B tile in the same K step can then take it from L1 instead of L2, which the core cannot see: it has no L1 for global loads (the tensor cache policy reaches only the L2 split). This would make the real loop faster than predicted, more where data is L2-resident (warm) than where the round trip is to DRAM (cold), as the residual is.
2. **A different round trip for `ld.global` than for the curves the core reads (tested here as a by-product).** The core's synchronous round trip comes from latency and bandwidth curves measured with `cp.async` and `ld.global.cg`. A default-cached `ld.global.v4` through L1 may differ even without reuse.
3. **Warps of a CTA out of step within a stage** (left for later): the barriers bound it, and nothing points to it yet.

## What is measured

A new mode, `sync-load-reuse`: the single-buffer register-staged loop of the kernels above (load A, barrier, load B, store both to shared memory, barrier, a fixed block of `ldmatrix` and `mma.sync` work), one stage of 16 KiB per CTA (8 KiB A, 8 KiB B), 4 warps, `grid` = CTAs per SM x 48. Each CTA records `%smid`, so co-residency is checked rather than assumed.

Arms, every combination of:

- `load`: `ld.global.v4` with the default cache operator, as Triton emits; `ld.global.cg.v4`, which bypasses L1.
- `sharing`: the CTAs on one SM read the same A and B addresses in each step (`shared`), or each reads its own (`disjoint`). Within a CTA the addresses are the same in both arms; only who else reads them changes.
- `ctas_per_sm`: 1 and 2 (`shared` and `disjoint` are the same at 1 CTA).
- `level`: L2-resident (a 4 MiB region, read before timing, as warm) and DRAM (a region of at least 16 times the L2 size, behind the read-only flush, as cold).

## Launch protocol

As every scheduled mode ([long-launch-capture.md](../../components/long-launch-capture.md)): pilot to 1.25 ms per launch, frozen schedule with the arms as design fields, every launch at least 1.0 ms, `--clock-probe`. 15 samples and 3 warm-ups. Spark 1 under the GPU lock after the quiet-host gate, three processes in one session, each host-checked; a failed host check voids that process, replaced once. A launch whose CTAs are not two per SM on every SM (from `%smid`) voids the point.

## Quantities

The time per step of each point: the median of its samples over `steps`, per process; over processes, the median of three.

## Frozen reading

At two CTAs per SM, per `level`:

1. **Capture rules**, as usual. A failing point is reported, not dropped.
2. **L1 reuse is shown** when, in all three processes, the default-cached `disjoint` step is at least 5% slower than the default-cached `shared` step, and the `.cg` `disjoint` and `shared` steps differ by less than 2%. **Not shown** when, in all three, the default-cached `shared` and `disjoint` steps differ by less than 2%. Otherwise not resolved.
3. Reading 2 at the L2-resident level decides candidate 1. The DRAM level is reported with the same reading and does not decide it.

At one CTA per SM, per `level`:

4. **The default-cached path differs** when, in all three processes, the default-cached and `.cg` steps differ by at least 5%; **does not** when they differ by less than 2% in all three; otherwise not resolved. This is reported for candidate 2 and does not decide candidate 1.

## Reported without a verdict

- For each `num_stages = 1` configuration of the archived v0.2 GB10 holdout IRs (opened data, CPU only): how many CTAs share an SM at the core's occupancy, and whether co-resident CTAs under the round-robin dispatch read the same A or B tiles. This says where the mechanism could act; no residual is fitted to it.
- The SM clock and per-cycle step times.

## Not covered

- More than two CTAs per SM, other stage sizes and warp counts.
- The RTX 4070: the same plan applies later, under the quiet desktop gate.
- How the core would model L1 for global loads.
