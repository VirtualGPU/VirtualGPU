## Reported without a verdict

- At the L2-resident level, with `shared` and default-cached loads, two CTAs per SM take 564 ns per step, close to the 541 ns of one CTA alone: the second CTA's loads cost little, as if served by L1. With `.cg`, or with `disjoint` addresses, two CTAs take 863 to 867 ns.
- At the DRAM level both load paths are about twice as fast `shared` as `disjoint`. In `shared` the SM's two CTAs read one region, so the GPU reads half as many distinct bytes from DRAM; with `.cg` too, the second read is served by L2, not DRAM. This is why the DRAM level cannot isolate L1 reuse, and the plan does not use it to decide.
- **Where it could act** (the plan's item on the archived IRs; opened data, CPU only, [coresidency-v02-gb10.json](v0.3-sync-load-reuse/coresidency-v02-gb10.json)). Of the 108 `num_stages = 1` configurations of the v0.2 GB10 GEMM holdout IRs, the core's occupancy on `profiles/gb10-0.23.0.json` puts more than one CTA per SM in 104 (2 to 10). Under the core's round-robin dispatch (CTA `c` on SM `c mod 48`), in 101 of them the co-resident CTAs of at least one SM read the same A or B tile in the first wave (A tiles only in 82, B tiles only in 4, both in 15), and in 94 every SM with co-resident CTAs does; where a shape has a single row of tiles (the decode shapes at their larger tile heights), every CTA reads the same A tile. This assumes the hardware dispatches and steps the CTAs as the core does, which was not measured.
- The forced carveout leaves an L1 smaller than what Triton's `num_stages = 1` kernels get (the amendment's stated cost), so this shows that the mechanism exists on GB10, not how much it saves in a real kernel. The RTX 4070 part waits for a quiet window.
